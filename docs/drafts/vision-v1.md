# Product Vision — v1 (superseded)

## Statement as it stood

**FOR** individuals and households in Costa Rica who manage money across colones and
dollars, **WHO** need to see where their money actually goes without hand-keying every
transaction into a spreadsheet, **THE** {{PRODUCT_NAME}} **is a** cross-platform personal
finance dashboard **THAT** consolidates spending, budgets and assets into one
currency-aware view, ingesting transactions automatically wherever a bank permits it and
gracefully by import or quick-entry wherever it does not. **UNLIKE** Mint-style trackers
built for single-currency US banking, or the personal-finance widget inside a single
bank's app, **OUR PRODUCT** treats multi-currency as a first-class concept and shows the
user their whole financial picture across every institution they use.

## What changed, and why

Replaced by v2. The UNLIKE clause named the wrong competitors. It was written against
US-market trackers and a bank's built-in personal-finance widget, neither of which the
intended user has actually tried to use and abandoned. The real alternatives being
displaced are a hand-maintained spreadsheet and a consumer tracker whose automatic
capture misfires — naming those makes the differentiation concrete rather than
theoretical.

The THAT clause was also too general. "Consolidates spending, budgets and assets into one
currency-aware view" describes the category, not this product. It says nothing about the
behaviours that actually distinguish it: capture at the moment of the charge, exclusion
of transfers between a person's own accounts, and valuation net of exit fees.
